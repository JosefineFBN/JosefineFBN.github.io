Two Crimes, One Pattern: The Hidden Patterns Between Assaults and Robberies in San Francisco

Drawing on two decades of data, this analysis uncovers how assault and robbery are interconnected across San Francisco's urban landscape. Want to know where it's safe to explore—and where to stay cautious? Read on.

The detection and prevention of crime is a field that has been continuersly modernised and especially revolutionized since the implementation of larger crime data analysis in the form of predictive police software. Crime data is now used in most american police districts to systematically organise the departments ressources for the best prevention. Such data can be used to show statistical correlation, and help the police notice patterns in the occurence of different types of crimes. The flip side of large scale use of predictive police software is the missing reasoning for the analytical conclusions. Data analysis is based on numbers and categories and the specific definitions therefore has a large influence of what category the specific crime is classified under. Furthermore, socioeconomic, incidents and seasonal occasions are just other factors that has a great influence in explaining crime patterns, but is something that can not be solely explained through data. It is therefore found interesting to look deeper into the relationship between different crime types, their occurence timewise and geographicly and further more their development the last twenty years, to help better understand what can be studied through data, and what needs to be further explained through other cultural and sociatal explanations. This article will explain such a study that will provide statistical analysis of crime data, while trying to compliment the explaining with other socio-economic evidence. The article will use a dataframe from the San Francisco Police Department with data concerning crime occurances from 2003-2026. To simplify the analysis it will be based on to crime types: Assault and Robbery. The reasoning for choosing these two crimes is that they at first glance are thought of as very similar. Robbery and assault both occurs more often in larger cities, can happen impulsively and is something that is associated with more socially vulnerable areas.
