Two Crimes, One Pattern: The Hidden Patterns Between Assaults and Robberies in San Francisco

Drawing on two decades of data, this analysis uncovers how assault and robbery are interconnected across San Francisco's urban landscape. Want to know where it's safe to explore—and where to stay cautious? Read on.

The detection and prevention of crime is a field that has been continuersly modernised and especially revolutionized since the implementation of larger crime data analysis in the form of predictive police software. Crime data is now used in most american police districts to systematically organise the departments ressources for the best prevention.
Such data can be used to show statistical correlation, and help the police notice patterns in the occurence of different types of crimes. The flip side of large scale use of predictive police software is the missing reasoning for the analytical conclusions. Data analysis is based on numbers and categories and the specific definitions therefore has a large influence of what category the specific crime is classified under. Furthermore, socioeconomic, incidents and seasonal occasions are just other factors that has a great influence in explaining crime patterns, but is something that can not be solely explained through data.
It is therefore found interesting to look deeper into the relationship between different crime types, their occurence timewise and geographicly and further more their development the last twenty years, to help better understand what can be studied through data, and what needs to be further explained through other cultural and sociatal explanations.
This article will explain such a study that will provide statistical analysis of crime data, while trying to compliment the explaining with other socio-economic evidence. The article will use a dataframe from the San Francisco Police Department with data concerning crime occurances from 2003-2026. To simplify the analysis it will be based on to crime types: Assault and Robbery. The reasoning for choosing these two crimes is that they at first glance are thought of as very similar. Robbery and assault both occurs more often in larger cities, can happen impulsively and is something that is associated with more socially vulnerable areas.

Where do Assault and Robbery occur across the City?

A natural question that arise when observing this linear correlation is where these crimes often occur and at what time. One might even ask which demographic groups are most commonly invovled in these crimes. Importantly, this latter question is just as relevant as the first ones, but also highlight the limitations of social data. however, what is possible to examine is if the linear correlation is because of the spatiale distribution of the two crimes. The figure below highlights the relative risk of Assault and Robbery in the districts of San fracisco, revealing interresting partterns but also differences. This map reflects the relative risk understood as relative to the registered crime categories within the dataset.

The relative risk of the occurance of incidents of Assualt and Robbery in San Fransisco. This is shown by data from two dacades covering incidents of Assault and Robbery in the districts of San francisco from 2003-2025.

Looking at the map, at first glance one might find that the spatial distributions between the two crimes are similar. In particular, it is fair to say that both crimes tend to occur in the south-eastern area of San Fracisco. For instance, looking at the district Tenderloin, both Assaults and Robberies occurs more frequently. This is not surprising as Terderloin has the highest crime rate in San Francisco and is known for its violent streets, despite its small size (San Francisco, n.d.).

In genereal, the southern and Eastern part of San Francisco are chatacyerized as more violent and rough neighborhoods whereas the eastern and western part of San Fracisco are considered more safe (Reolink, 2026). The frecuency of assaults and robberies are following this overall trends.
However, taking a closer look noteable differences appear. While robbery occurs more frequently in the district Ingleside, the frequency of Assault remain the same level within the district.

As we are examine the reason for the strong linear correlation between Assault and Robbery, it is important to emphasize that conclusion can not soley be made upon the spatial distribution.
